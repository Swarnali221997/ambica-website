What is galva flux and why does it matter?

Galvanizing4 min readAmbica Chemicals
Zn

In hot-dip galvanizing, steel is dipped in molten zinc. For the zinc to bond, the steel surface has to be perfectly clean at the moment it enters the bath. Flux is what keeps it that way.

The job flux does

After pickling and rinsing, steel is exposed to air and starts to oxidise again within minutes. A flux layer applied before the dip cleans off the last traces of oxide, shields the surface until it meets the zinc, and helps the molten zinc wet the steel evenly.

What it is made of

Galvanizing fluxes are typically built on zinc chloride and ammonium chloride, the same two chemicals Ambica Chemicals manufactures in Hooghly. Because the flux is only as good as its salts, consistent purity matters more than it first appears.

A clean, consistent flux means fewer rejected coatings and less zinc wasted as dross.

What to check when you buy

  • Form. Crystal, powder or solution, matched to how your line mixes it.
  • Consistency. Batch-to-batch quality, backed by lab testing at the supplier.
  • Packing. Sealed, clean packing so the material arrives dry and uncontaminated.
  • Availability. Ready stock, so a line is never held up waiting on material.
